I do not want this site to be only a profile. I want it to become a place where my research questions, reading notes, projects, and unfinished thoughts can gradually accumulate.

## The starting point

The first reason for building this website is simple: I need a stable place to put things. In the past, many thoughts stayed in chat windows, notes, temporary documents, or scattered folders. They were useful at that moment, but after a few weeks I often could not easily find them again, and I could not see how one thought was connected to another.

A personal website gives these materials an address. It does not need to be perfect from the beginning. What matters more is that it can keep changing with me. When my interests become clearer, the homepage can become clearer. When a project becomes more concrete, the project page can grow with it. When a vague thought becomes a research note, it can move from a private draft to a blog post.

## More than a CV

A CV records results: education, papers, projects, skills. Those are important, but they are only the visible part. For someone still learning how to do research, the invisible part may be even more important: how I choose a problem, how I read a paper, how I get stuck, how I revise my understanding, and how a loose question slowly becomes a more precise one.

This is why I want the blog to exist. Blog posts can hold the middle part of research: not yet polished enough to be a paper, but already useful enough to record. A short reading reflection, a failed experiment, a proof idea that did not work, a question that keeps bothering me: all of these can become part of the site.

## A place to make thinking more concrete

Writing for a public page forces me to slow down. If a sentence cannot be written clearly, it usually means that the thought behind it is still vague. If a research interest can only be expressed as a list of keywords, it means I have not yet understood the problem deeply enough. The website becomes a gentle pressure: it asks me to explain myself in a way that another person can follow.

I do not expect every post to be profound. Some posts may simply be records of confusion. But even confusion can be useful if it is written honestly and carefully. A clear record of where I got stuck may later become a clue for a better question.

## How I hope to use it

For now, I imagine three kinds of updates. The first is research notes: what I am reading, what I understand, and what remains unclear. The second is project logs: what I tried, what worked, what failed, and what I would change next time. The third is reflective writing: how I understand research training, communication, tools, and the habits that make long-term work possible.

The long-term goal is modest but important: when someone opens this site, they should not only know my name and affiliation, but also get a sense of what I care about, how I think, what I am building, and what kind of questions I am learning to ask.
